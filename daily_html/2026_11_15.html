Training Reasoning Models in Simulation to Natively Use Real Robots VLA

robot-use reinforcement learning sim-to-real transfer reasoning model open-weight vision-language-action

简要说明：本文介绍了PEAR，一种完全在仿真中通过机器人使用强化学习训练开源的27B推理模型，使其原生控制真实机器人，在真实双臂机器人和新任务上取得高成功率，且无需演示。

TLDR: This paper introduces PEAR, a post-training engine that uses robot-use reinforcement learning entirely in simulation to train an open-weight 27B reasoning model to natively control real robots, achieving high success rates on real dual-arm robots and novel tasks without demonstrations.

Abstract: Robot-use agents (RUAs) have emerged as a promising approach to general-purpose robot control. However, strong performance has so far been achieved only by frontier proprietary models such as GPT-6 Astra and Claude Fable 5.2, whereas open-weight models remain substantially less capable. We show that an open-weight 27B reasoning model can be trained to use real robots natively through reinforcement learning, entirely in simulation and without demonstrations. Like a computer-use agent (CUA), the model observes the environment, reasons in a chain of thought, and generates the next end-effector pose, without any harness. We build a Post-training Engine for Agentic Robotics (PEAR) and use it to train the model on simulated robot tasks at scale with robot-use reinforcement learning (RURL), mirroring how frontier models acquire computer use. We find that the skills acquired in simulation generalize well beyond the training tasks: they transfer to an unseen robot, to the real world, and to novel instructions. After training on LIBERO and RoboDojo in simulation, the model succeeds in 60% of our trials on a real dual-arm robot, whereas the base model succeeds in none. On LIBERO, training raises the success rate from 1.6% to 74.4%, approaching GPT-6 Astra and GPT-6.1 Sol at maximum reasoning effort. Compared with $π_{0.5}$, a vision-language-action model fine-tuned on demonstrations, our model is substantially more robust to changes in the task itself. On LIBERO-Pro, $π_{0.5}$ succeeds in only 0.75% of episodes when assigned a new task in a familiar scene whereas our model succeeds in 57.4%. Finally, the model retains its general capabilities, with no measurable degradation on nine reasoning, coding and vision benchmarks. These results highlight robot-use reinforcement learning as a promising component of foundation-model post-training.

When Will You Hand It Over? A Hidden Start Delay Makes Self-Play Handover Robust to Unfamiliar Partners RLRobot

self-play hidden start-delay randomization handover humanoid robot robustness

简要说明：本文发现自博弈交接策略在面对不熟悉伙伴时会失效，原因是学习到的给予者总是立即开始递出物品；通过引入隐藏起始延迟随机化（HSD），使接收者对未见过的伙伴更加鲁棒，成功率从31%提升到80%。

TLDR: The paper identifies that self-play handover policies for humanoid robots break with unfamiliar partners due to learned partners always starting offers immediately, and introduces hidden start-delay randomization (HSD) to make the receiver robust to unseen partners, improving success from 31% to 80% without retraining the partner.

Abstract: Cooperative self-play can teach a humanoid to receive an object without any human data, but the partner it meets after training is never the one it trained with; we show that a fluent self-play handover policy breaks with unfamiliar partners, identify the main cause, and repair it. In a physics-based parcel handover between a Unitree G1 and a learned giver, both trained by alternating PPO self-play, the receiver completes 95% and 90% of handovers with its own partner (two training seeds) but only 31% with scripted partners it has never met. Paired interventions isolate the cause: under a shared reward the learned giver always starts its offer at once, so the receiver learns a single rendezvous, and delaying its own partner by one second cuts success from 95% to 6% (87% to 34% in the second seed). We introduce hidden start-delay randomisation (HSD), a one-line rule on the learned partner that the receiver never observes. After 300 further updates, success with unseen scripted partners rises from 31% to 80% in both seeds, against 35--41% for a budget-matched control. The gain holds on a fresh scenario seed, on eleven held-out partner conditions and on partners that start later than any training delay; it cuts high-force contact three- to five-fold and needs no retraining of the partner.

Disturbance-Aware Informative Planning in Spatiotemporal Environments RLRobot

informative planning disturbance learning spatiotemporal monitoring trajectory optimization belief incorporation

简要说明：本文提出了一种两阶段信息规划框架，沿监测轨迹学习空间变化的扰动，并将其纳入轨迹优化，以提高时空环境中的信息采集效率。

TLDR: This paper proposes a two-stage informative planning framework that learns spatially varying disturbances along monitoring trajectories and incorporates them into trajectory optimization to improve information acquisition in spatiotemporal environments.

Abstract: Autonomous robots are widely used for persistent monitoring of spatiotemporal environments, where quantities of interest (QoI) change across space and time and require repeated measurements. In practice, robot motion can be affected by external disturbances that vary across the environment. When such external disturbances are not considered in trajectory optimization, it can lead to inaccurate predictions of robot motion and, consequently, the information acquired about the QoI during execution. However, estimating disturbances throughout the environment can be inefficient, as disturbances in regions with little overlap with monitoring trajectories have limited impact on monitoring performance. We therefore propose a two-stage informative planning framework for unknown, spatially varying but time-invariant disturbances, combining informed disturbance sampling with disturbance-aware trajectory optimization. First, we identify where QoI measurements are needed and generate alternative paths to these locations using the environment geometry. We then prioritize disturbance measurements based on their expected reduction of disturbance uncertainty along these paths. Second, rather than optimizing information acquisition based on nominal robot motion, we incorporate the learned disturbance belief into trajectory optimization to account for its effects on robot motion and information acquisition. Across four simulation scenarios, the proposed method reduces disturbance-learning time by 25.0--52.3 percent compared with environment-wide estimation and identifies paths that appear costly under nominal dynamics but become preferable when disturbances are considered.

摘要（中文）：自主机器人被广泛应用于时空环境的持续监测，其中关注量随空间和时间变化，需要重复测量。在实践中，机器人运动可能受到随环境变化的外部扰动影响。若在轨迹优化中未考虑此类外部扰动，可能导致对机器人运动的预测不准确，进而影响执行过程中获取的关注量信息。然而，对整个环境中的扰动进行估计可能效率低下，因为与监测轨迹重叠较少的区域中的扰动对监测性能的影响有限。为此，我们提出了一种针对未知、空间变化但时间不变的扰动的两阶段信息规划框架，将知情扰动采样与扰动感知轨迹优化相结合。首先，我们确定需要关注量测量的位置，并利用环境几何生成到达这些位置的替代路径。随后，我们根据扰动测量沿这些路径对扰动不确定性的预期减少量，对其优先级进行排序。其次，我们不基于名义机器人运动优化信息采集，而是将学习到的扰动信念融入轨迹优化中，以考虑其对机器人运动和信息采集的影响。在四个仿真场景中，与全环境估计方法相比，所提方法将扰动学习时间减少了25.0%至52.3%，并识别出在名义动力学下看似成本较高、但在考虑扰动后更优的路径。
