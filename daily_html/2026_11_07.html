Failure Prediction and Classification in Vision-Language-Action Models Via Calibrated Latent Probing VLA

failure detection latent probing calibration vision-language-action models manipulation

简要说明：本文提出了VLA-Tug，一种用于视觉-语言-动作模型的轻量级潜在探针，能够改善早期故障检测，并提供漏检率和误报率的有界保证，同时预测故障模式以支持实时机器人操作中的纠正动作。

TLDR: This paper introduces VLA-Tug, a lightweight latent probe for Vision-Language-Action models that improves early failure detection and provides calibrated bounds on missed-detection and false-alarm rates, while also predicting failure modes to enable corrective actions during real-time robot manipulation.

Abstract: Vision-Language-Action models (VLAs) enable general-purpose embodied intelligence, but their performance is brittle under distribution shift, where unanticipated failures can damage equipment or endanger people. Deploying VLAs in real-world systems therefore requires a runtime monitor that is timely, calibrated, and informative while being efficient enough to run on the edge. Existing methods fail to fulfill these requirements. We present VLA-Tug, a lightweight probe over the latent layers of a VLA backbone that improves early failure detection and bounds the missed-detection and false-alarm rates, with minimal inference overhead. We show that supervising on the pre-failure segment of a trajectory increases early failure detection at matched false-positive rates by up to 30% over the strongest learned baseline. Under shift to unseen tasks, the bounds on missed-detection and false-alarm rates remain valid in up to 86% of runs, versus at most 21% for baselines. Unlike existing methods, VLA-Tug predicts the failure mode to enable corrective actions, raising top-3 accuracy on unseen tasks by up to 44% over a majority baseline. VLA-Tug enables real-time monitoring where the well-timed and informative probe supports lightweight steering that improves task success. We release a dataset of 3,180 annotated manipulation episodes to support future work on latent failure detection in VLAs at https://vla-tug.github.io.

OopsieRecover: Learning Transferable VLA Corrections from Semantic Interventions VLA

VLA corrections abduction semantic interventions cost learning robotic manipulation

简要说明：OopsieRecover 通过溯因推理从少量纠正演示中推断意图，并将其编码为密集空间表示和条件成本，使VLA策略能够从失败中恢复并将纠正推广到新物体和场景。

TLDR: OopsieRecover uses abduction to infer intent from a few corrective demonstrations, encoding them as dense spatial representations and conditional costs to make VLA policies recover from failures and generalize corrections to novel objects and scenes.

Abstract: Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models can perform a wide range of robotic manipulation tasks, but often fail during deployment. Teaching them to recover from these failures requires substantial corrective data: existing approaches learn to recover from failures by directly associating corrective actions with the observations in which they were demonstrated. For instance, DAgger-like fine-tuning can require tens to hundreds of interactions, yet the resulting policy may still fail to generalize the learned corrections beyond the demonstrated scenarios. We hypothesize that semantics can capture the underlying reasons for failures and their corrections, enabling policies to learn from only a handful of corrective demonstrations and generalize those corrections to novel objects, and scene variations. We propose OopsieRecover, a framework that uses abduction to infer the intent behind a small set of corrective demonstrations and encodes the inferred knowledge as dense spatial representations and conditional costs. These costs capture when a behavior is undesirable and steer the policy toward more desirable action regions. We experiment across 6 tasks and variations in object poses, scenes, and related tasks, and find that only a handful of corrective demonstrations can enable a VLA to overcome failures across a broad range of novel situations, without requiring new corrective data for each variation. Please refer to https://robin-lab.cs.utexas.edu/oopsierecover for videos.

Latent RL Priors for Data-Efficient Dexterous Manipulation Manipulation

dexterous manipulation latent RL prior flow-matching action decoder visual adaptation data-efficient policy learning

简要说明：本文提出潜在RL先验（Latent RL Priors）两阶段框架，先将特权强化学习控制知识蒸馏到流匹配动作解码器中，再学习视觉接口将图像映射到潜在上下文，实现数据高效的灵巧操作，在低数据量下优于全参数微调基线。

TLDR: The paper introduces Latent RL Priors, a two-stage framework that distills privileged RL control knowledge into a flow-matching action decoder and then learns a visual interface to map images to the latent context, enabling data-efficient visual dexterous manipulation and outperforming full-parameter fine-tuning baselines in low-data regimes.

Abstract: Learning dexterous manipulation from limited visual demonstrations is challenging because policies must acquire both perceptual grounding and precise control, while reinforcement learning (RL) in simulation can acquire rich control behaviors from large-scale interaction with privileged information. We introduce \textbf{Latent RL Prior}, a two-stage framework that reuses this control knowledge for data-efficient visual policy learning. During privileged pretraining, we distill large-scale RL rollouts into a flow-matching action decoder conditioned on proprioception and latent task context. During visual adaptation, we freeze the decoder and learn a visual interface that maps images to its latent context using action supervision alone, without privileged labels or latent targets. The same decoder can further be shared across arms by learning coordinated visual contexts for bimanual manipulation. Across three simulated tasks, our method outperforms full-parameter fine-tuning of $π_{0.5}$ and GR00T N1.7 in the low-data regime; on door lifting and bimanual assembly, it surpasses full-data baselines using $10\times$ and $5\times$ fewer demonstrations, respectively. Further analysis shows that direct visual-to-latent conditioning improves adaptation performance and training stability over routing visual predictions through the pretrained state encoder. Robustness to unseen mass variation and greater linear accessibility of contact-force information in decoder representations further provide evidence of retained RL-acquired control knowledge. Project website: https://yixiaowang7.github.io/latent-rl-prior-page/.

T3R: Training-Free Two-Stage Token Refinement Towards Efficient and Robust VLA Models VLA

VLA token pruning attention biasing robustness efficiency

简要说明：T3R 提出了一种无需训练的两阶段 token 细化框架，通过剪枝无关视觉 token 并偏置注意力到关键区域，提升 VLA 模型的效率与鲁棒性。

TLDR: T3R proposes a training-free two-stage token refinement framework that prunes irrelevant visual tokens and biases attention toward action-critical regions to improve efficiency and robustness of VLA models.

Abstract: Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models enable end-to-end robot control, but inference is increasingly expensive. Each decision processes hundreds of visual tokens, often across multiple cameras, leading to substantial computation while allowing irrelevant background content to distract action prediction under distribution shift. We argue that these issues stem from two complementary forms of redundancy: many visual tokens are spatially irrelevant, while attention over the remaining tokens is often misallocated. Based on this observation, we propose T3R, a Training-Free Two-Stage Token Refinement framework. In the first stage, segmentation-guided token pruning removes irrelevant visual patches to reduce computation and eliminate distracting context. In the second stage, saliency-guided attention biasing reallocates attention toward action-critical visual regions and language instructions within the retained tokens. Together, pruning produces a cleaner candidate set, while attention biasing further emphasizes the most decision-relevant evidence, yielding a better accuracy-efficiency frontier than either stage alone. On LIBERO-Spatial, T3R removes 85% of scene-camera visual tokens while essentially matching the unpruned success rate, and improves success by up to 7 percentage points under out-of-distribution object-placement perturbations. The timing comparison attributes 7.8 ms per inference call to pruning and attention-bias application. As input resolution increases, LLM-forward profiling shows up to a 3.3x speedup over the unpruned baseline. Evaluations across LIBERO, SIMPLER, and RoboTwin with three VLA backbones show the strongest accuracy-efficiency benefits in multi-camera settings. These results demonstrate that training-free token refinement is a practical approach to efficient and robust VLA inference.

摘要（中文）：视觉-语言-动作（VLA）模型实现了端到端的机器人控制，但其推理成本日益高昂。每次决策需处理数百个视觉令牌，且通常来自多个摄像头，这不仅导致大量计算开销，还会在分布偏移下使无关背景内容干扰动作预测。我们认为，这些问题源于两种互补形式的冗余：大量视觉令牌在空间上无关，而对剩余令牌的注意力分配常常失衡。基于这一观察，我们提出T3R，一种无需训练的两阶段令牌精炼框架。第一阶段，基于分割引导的令牌剪枝移除无关视觉区域，以减少计算量并消除干扰性上下文；第二阶段，基于显著性引导的注意力偏置在保留令牌中重新分配注意力，使其聚焦于动作关键视觉区域和语言指令。二者结合，剪枝生成更干净的候选集，注意力偏置则进一步强调与决策最相关的证据，相比任一单独阶段均实现更优的精度-效率平衡。在LIBERO-Spatial上，T3R移除了85%的场景摄像头视觉令牌，同时基本保持未剪枝的成功率，并且在分布外物体放置扰动下将成功率提升最多7个百分点。时间对比显示，每次推理调用中剪枝和注意力偏置应用各占7.8毫秒。随着输入分辨率增加，LLM前向分析显示，相比未剪枝基线最高可实现3.3倍的加速。在LIBERO、SIMPLER和RoboTwin上的评估以及三种VLA骨干网络的测试表明，多摄像头设置下精度-效率收益最为显著。这些结果证明，无需训练的令牌精炼是高效且鲁棒VLA推理的一种实用方法。

Social Humanoid: Socially Responsive Human-Humanoid Interaction HumanoidEmbodied

human-humanoid interaction whole-body motion generation hierarchical policy adaptive retargeting socially responsive behavior

简要说明：Social Humanoid 提出了一种分层系统，使29自由度的仿人机器人能够通过预测和响应人类伙伴来执行社交响应全身运动，并利用自适应重定向生成的新基准进行训练，在仿真和真实世界中得到验证。

TLDR: Social Humanoid introduces a hierarchical system enabling a 29-DoF humanoid robot to perform socially responsive whole-body motion by predicting and reacting to a human partner, trained on a new 260-hour human-humanoid interaction benchmark via adaptive retargeting, and validated in simulation and real world.

Abstract: Humanoid robots are typically trained as solitary agents, leaving them unable to coordinate physically with human partners who naturally anticipate and adjust to one another. To bridge this gap, we present Social Humanoid, a hierarchical system that generates and executes socially responsive whole-body motion on a 29-DoF Unitree G1 conditioned on a human partner's motion. Its high-level policy performs flexible sequence modeling of human and humanoid motion through a shared tokenized representation, generating reactive humanoid motion and anticipating the partner's motion when they are occluded, while a low-level whole-body tracking controller executes the generated motion on the robot. To train the system, we introduce a two-phase adaptive retargeting procedure that retargets one interaction partner to the robot while morphing the other partner to preserve contact dynamics, reachability, and relative body scale. We apply this procedure across five motion-capture datasets to produce a new Human-Humanoid Interaction (HHI) benchmark of 260 hours of human-humanoid interactions. Our results demonstrate that Social Humanoid produces coordinated, human-aligned behavior across diverse interaction types in simulation and the real world. Project page: https://von31.github.io/social-humanoid/

VFlash: Fast, Accurate Visual Planning via Flash Primitive Decoding VLA

visual planning flash primitive decoding autoregressive vision-language model world action model robot trajectory

简要说明：VFlash 通过轻量级草稿模型一次性预测完整视觉原语（点、框、轨迹），加速视觉规划，提升机器人轨迹任务的空间精度和生成速度，并提升世界动作模型的成功率。

TLDR: VFlash accelerates visual planning in vision-language models by using a lightweight drafter to predict entire visual primitives (points, boxes, trajectories) in one forward pass, improving spatial accuracy and speed for robot trajectory tasks and world action model conditioning.

Abstract: Models for physical AI tasks, such as robotics and autonomous driving, need not reason solely in language. They can use visual plans--points, bounding boxes, and trajectories--to represent spatial goals and anticipate motion. However, autoregressive vision-language models generate these plans one coordinate token at a time, making richer predictions increasingly costly. We introduce VFlash, a method for fast, accurate visual planning through Flash Primitive Decoding. VFlash exploits the known structure of visual primitives to predict their coordinates jointly, using a lightweight drafter that generates an entire primitive in one forward pass. The layout adapts to the primitive's type, dimensionality, and length, supporting compact boxes and long, variable-length trajectories. Crucially, spatial accuracy need not require reproducing an autoregressive model's exact token sequence. VFlash therefore trains on annotated primitives and directly accepts structurally valid predictions without target-token agreement. Across the evaluated 2D and 3D robot trajectory tasks, VFlash consistently reduces prediction errors relative to autoregressive decoding. Preliminary single-rollout measurements indicate approximately 25x faster generation for 3D point motion forecasting. A world action model conditioned on VFlash plans achieves 70.2% average success across four MolmoSpaces task suites, compared with 62.6% for the baseline.

SCALE: Real-Time Latent Communication for Cyber-Physical Agents Other

latent communication cyber-physical agents real-time scheduling bandwidth constraints multi-agent collaboration

简要说明：SCALE 提出了一种针对物理信息融合系统的实时潜空间通信方法，通过 token 匹配和调度器在带宽受限下减少冗余传输、满足任务截止时间，从而提升多智能体机器人系统的性能。

TLDR: SCALE introduces a real-time latent communication method for cyber-physical agents, using token matching and a scheduler to reduce bandwidth usage while meeting task deadlines, improving performance in multi-agent robotic systems.

Abstract: Latent communication enables agents to collaborate efficiently by directly exchanging intermediate model representations rather than raw sensor data or text messages. Existing approaches, however, are primarily designed for LLM agents and do not consider the bandwidth constraints and heterogeneous task deadlines faced by cyber-physical agents. In this work, we propose SCALE, a real-time latent communication method tailored for cyber-physical agents. Motivated by the spatio-temporal continuity of the physical world, SCALE matches tokens across consecutive latent messages using similarity scores to identify reusable information and avoid redundant transmission. It then profiles the semantic gain and communication cost of transmitting different amounts of latent information. Based on these profiles, SCALE employs a real-time scheduler that jointly determines which pending messages to transmit and how many latent tokens to send, subject to available bandwidth and task deadlines. We evaluate SCALE across three cyber-physical benchmarks and a physical spider robot case study under diverse network conditions and heterogeneous task deadlines.
