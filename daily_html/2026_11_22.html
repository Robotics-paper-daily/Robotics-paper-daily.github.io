Task2Hand: A Contact-Grounded Skill for Parametric Dexterous-Hand Morphology Design Manipulation

dexterous hand design contact-grounded parametric morphology skill specification simulation

简要说明：Task2Hand 提出了一种基于接触的技能规范，可自主生成、验证和训练用于操作任务的灵巧手形态，相比基线手提高了成功率。

TLDR: Task2Hand introduces a contact-grounded skill specification that autonomously generates, validates, and trains dexterous hand morphologies for manipulation tasks, improving success rates over baseline hands.

Abstract: Dexterous hand design demands repeated expert work across geometry, robot-description code, collision debugging, grasp initialization, and policy training. We present Task2Hand, a Skill: a specification package that turns a functional manipulation brief into a parameterized hand model and routes every candidate through cost-ordered contact, dynamics, and learning gates. Each gate returns a status and an evidence record; on failure, the Skill records the earliest blocker, revises one bounded parameter group, and reruns the cheaper gates. The agent generates, validates, and trains autonomously within declared bounds, and escalates trade-offs, plateaus, and conflicting evidence to a human who sets the next design direction. An evidence-driven design trajectory for bidirectional in-hand manipulation yielded SAGE Hand (Skill-Aware Generative Embodiment): four fingers, 16 actuated DoF, an integrated guiding palm, compact roots, cylindrical tips, and a collision-cleared opposable thumb. Under a matched training protocol, SAGE Hand reaches 45.6% task success on a hexagonal prism against 37.3% for the LEAP hand, and 70.8% on a bicolor cylinder against 51.6%. Dense simulated tactile input more than halves the cylinder drop rate. In a controlled iteration study, the Skill path preserves capture at 164/165 trials while the path without the Skill degrades to 14/165, confirming that the gates act as a regression guard during refinement. Accepted candidates pack with URDF/MJCF, metrics, checkpoints, and a provenance manifest.

What Must a Model Tell a Planner? Outcome Interfaces for Risk-Aware Planning After Robot Damage Locomotion

outcome model capability contract risk-aware planning robot damage locomotion

简要说明：本文研究了机器人受损后，学习模型的决策接口如何影响风险感知的路径规划，提出了一种预测每种地形成功概率和通行时间的“能力契约”结果模型，并证明在风险约束下比决策策略更能保留任务。

TLDR: The paper studies how the decision interface of learned models affects risk-aware route planning after robot damage, proposing a 'capability contract' outcome model that predicts success chance and travel time per terrain, and showing it outperforms decision policies in preserving missions under risk constraints.

Abstract: When a robot is damaged, it must decide which routes it can still afford, and the amount of risk it may accept can change from one mission to the next. We argue that whether a learned model helps such a robot depends on its decision interface, that is, on what the model hands to the planner. A decision policy tells the planner what to do, whereas an outcome model, a kind of world model, predicts the consequences of each route. We study simulated Unitree Go2 quadrupeds and H1 humanoids with weakened motors, added payloads, or reduced foot friction. We compare four interfaces. The first is a decision policy, and the second is the same policy trained with fixed penalties for infeasible routes. The third is an outcome model that predicts the feasibility and travel time of each route. The fourth, which we call a capability contract, is an outcome model that predicts the chance of success and the travel time of the robot on each terrain and composes them along a route. All share the same collected experience, matched tuning and risk calibration, and an uncertainty guard. Two comparatively low-risk probes let the capability contract rank hazardous terrains about as well as four new trials on every hazard. At a calibrated 5% limit on selecting empirically infeasible routes, outcome models cut declined missions from about 32% to 7-8% on Go2 and from 57% to 35-36% on H1 compared with the plain policy. Fixed penalties narrow this gap, but the best penalty changes with the risk target. Predicting outcomes lets the planner preserve missions by changing routes as risk requirements tighten.

摘要（中文）：当机器人受损时，它必须判断自身仍能承担哪些路线，且其可接受的风险程度会随任务不同而变化。我们认为，学习模型能否对此类机器人有所帮助，取决于其决策接口，即模型向规划器传递的内容。决策策略告知规划器应执行何种操作，而结果模型——一种世界模型——则预测每条路线可能产生的后果。本研究以模拟的Unitree Go2四足机器人和H1人形机器人为对象，分别设置电机弱化、负载增加或足部摩擦降低等条件，比较四种接口方案。第一种是决策策略；第二种是在该策略基础上，对不可行路线施加固定惩罚进行训练；第三种是预测每条路线可行性与通行时间的结果模型；第四种被称为“能力契约”，这是一种结果模型，能够预测机器人在每种地形上的成功概率和通行时间，并沿路线对其进行组合。所有方案均共享相同的经验数据、匹配的参数调优与风险校准，并配有不确定性防护机制。两个相对低风险的探针任务使得“能力契约”对危险地形的排序能力，几乎等同于对每种危险地形进行四次全新试验的效果。在设定为5%的经验不可行路线选择校准限制下，与普通策略相比，结果模型将Go2上被放弃的任务比例从约32%降至7%至8%，将H1上的比例从57%降至35%至36%。固定惩罚虽缩小了这一差距，但最优惩罚值随风险目标而变化。预测结果使规划器能够在风险要求收紧时，通过调整路线来保留任务。

Non-penetrating World Models WorldModel

简要说明：本文提出了NoPeBench基准来评估视频世界模型中的物体穿透问题，并提出了NoPeWorld框架，通过冻结DiT的隐藏状态训练一个轻量级预测头来检测并替换不安全动作，在六个世界模型上将物理违反率从74%降至16%。

TLDR: This paper introduces NoPeBench, a benchmark to evaluate and understand object penetration failures in interactive video world models, and proposes NoPeWorld, a lightweight framework that uses a prediction head on frozen DiT hidden states to detect and replace unsafe actions, reducing physical violations from 74% to 16% across six world models.

Abstract: We study a new problem of preventing object penetration in interactive video world models. To understand how existing models handle this, we introduce NoPeBench, a benchmark that evaluates how world models respond to physical obstacles. Our evaluation reveals that none of the open-source models can reliably prevent such failures. Surprisingly, however, these models already encode the information needed to predict such violations. We find that the hidden states of diffusion transformers (DiTs) can predict whether a candidate action will penetrate an obstacle. Building on this, we introduce NoPeWorld, a lightweight framework that detects and prevents penetration. Specifically, we train a small prediction head on top of a frozen DiT's hidden states to read out if a candidate action is safe or not. Unsafe actions are then replaced with safe ones that best preserve the original intent. Despite its simplicity, NoPeWorld substantially reduces the physical violation rate, averaged over six video world models, from 74% to 16%. Remarkably, our method shows zero-shot generalization to open-domain imagery, third-person viewpoints, and moving obstacles. We publicly release our code, the NoPeBench benchmark, and model weights. Project Page: https://nopeworld.github.io .

摘要（中文）：我们研究了一个新问题：如何在交互式视频世界模型中防止物体穿透。为了理解现有模型如何处理这一问题，我们引入了NoPeBench，一个评估世界模型对物理障碍物反应的基准。评估结果显示，目前没有任何开源模型能够可靠地避免此类穿透失败。然而，令人惊讶的是，这些模型其实已经编码了预测此类违规所需的信息。我们发现，扩散变压器（DiTs）的隐藏状态能够预测某个候选动作是否会导致穿透。基于此，我们提出了NoPeWorld，一个轻量级框架，用于检测并防止穿透。具体而言，我们在冻结的DiT隐藏状态之上训练一个轻量预测头，以判断候选动作是否安全。不安全的动作将被替换为最能保留原始意图的安全动作。尽管方法简单，NoPeWorld在六个视频世界模型上平均将物理违规率从74%降至16%。值得注意的是，我们的方法在开放域图像、第三人称视角和移动障碍物场景中展现出零样本泛化能力。我们已公开发布代码、NoPeBench基准和模型权重。项目主页：https://nopeworld.github.io。

Interactive Semi-Parametric On-Track Learning for Vehicle Dynamics in Autonomous Racing AutonomousDriving

Autonomous racing Vehicle dynamics Semi-parametric learning on-track learning prediction-error

简要说明：本文提出了一种交互式半参数学习框架，在自主赛车中联合调整物理参数和残差模型，确保共享预测误差目标非增。仿真和硬件实验表明，与标称模型相比，侧向速度和横摆角速度预测误差显著降低。

TLDR: This paper introduces an interactive semi-parametric learning framework that jointly adapts physical parameters and a learned residual model for vehicle dynamics in autonomous racing, ensuring a non-increasing shared prediction-error objective. Simulations and hardware experiments show significant reductions in lateral velocity and yaw rate prediction errors compared to a nominal model.

Abstract: Autonomous racing requires an accurate vehicle dynamics model near the limits of handling, yet a mismatch between the nominal and actual dynamics inevitably remains. To reduce this mismatch, existing methods learn from on-track data, i.e., data collected while the vehicle drives on the track under racing conditions, by adapting the physical parameters of the nominal model or by learning a residual that captures the remaining mismatch. However, these on-track learning methods typically handle either the physical parameters or the residual separately, which restricts their ability to resolve the mismatch. To address this limitation, we propose an interactive semi-parametric learning framework that learns both the physical parameters and the residual under a single shared prediction-error objective. Since updating one changes the learning target of the other, they are recursively updated in turn, each guided by the other's latest update. Each update is then accepted only if it does not increase the shared objective, so that the objective is guaranteed to be non-increasing and convergent over the recursion. Numerical simulations and hardware experiments show that the proposed framework outperforms the baselines. Compared with the nominal model, it reduces the one-step prediction errors of lateral velocity and yaw rate by 90.0% and 81.6% in simulation and by 54.0% and 59.4% on hardware.

摘要（中文）：自主赛车需要在操控极限附近具备精确的车辆动力学模型，然而名义动力学与实际动力学之间的失配始终难以避免。为减小这一失配，现有方法通过利用赛道数据——即在赛车工况下车辆在赛道上行驶时采集的数据——来学习，具体方式包括调整名义模型的物理参数，或学习一个用于捕捉残余失配的残差项。然而，这些基于赛道数据的学习方法通常分别处理物理参数或残差项，限制了其解决失配问题的能力。为克服这一局限，我们提出了一种交互式半参数学习框架，在统一的共享预测误差目标下同时学习物理参数和残差项。由于更新其中一项会改变另一项的学习目标，二者在递归过程中交替更新，各自以对方的最新更新为指引。每次更新仅在不会增大共享目标的前提下被接受，从而保证该目标在递归过程中单调不增并收敛。数值仿真与硬件实验表明，所提出的框架优于基线方法。与名义模型相比，在仿真中，侧向速度和横摆角速度的单步预测误差分别降低了90.0%和81.6%；在硬件实验中，分别降低了54.0%和59.4%。

Real-time neuromorphic audio and vision perception for autonomous robotic applications: Integration and experimental evaluation on a rover platform Other

neuromorphic computing spiking neural network event-based camera ROS 2 mobile robot

简要说明：本文介绍了一款四轮巡逻车，将神经形态音频（SynSense Xylo上的SNN）和事件相机视觉（SpikeYOLO）流水线与经典PANN音频标签相结合，均以ROS 2容器化服务部署，并在实时概念验证场景中进行了演示。

TLDR: This paper presents a four-wheel rover integrating neuromorphic audio (SNN on SynSense Xylo) and event-based vision (SpikeYOLO) pipelines with classical PANN audio tagging, all deployed as containerized ROS 2 services and demonstrated in a real-time proof-of-concept scenario.

Abstract: Neuromorphic sensing and computing offer a promising alternative to conventional deep learning pipelines for mobile robots, where perception must run continuously under tight latency and energy constraints. However, most neuromorphic solutions are still evaluated offline or on isolated benchmarks, and their integration into complete robotic systems remains largely unexplored. In this technical report, a four-wheel rover platform is presented that combines three real-time perception pipelines: a classical audio tagging pipeline based on Pretrained Audio Neural Networks (PANNs) running on an NVIDIA Jetson GPU, a neuromorphic audio pipeline in which a spiking neural network (SNN) is deployed on the SynSense Xylo Audio~2 chip, and a neuromorphic vision pipeline in which events from an event-based camera are processed by the SpikeYOLO spiking detector for traffic cone detection. On the Xylo chip, the whole processing chain---from the microphone signal to the classification of low- and high-pitched sounds---is performed on-chip, while the host only configures the device and reads the output neurons. All models are deployed as containerised services and integrated through ROS~2, and a configurable action dispatcher maps the detected classes to robot behaviours, closing the loop between perception and actuation. The platform is demonstrated in a proof-of-concept scenario in which the robot reacts to sound classes detected by both audio pipelines and detects traffic cones with the event camera, showing the feasibility of integrating neuromorphic hardware into a multimodal robotic platform operating in real time.
