SaiVLA-JEPA: Evaluating Future Latent Prediction in JEPA-Conditioned VLA Control VLA

VLA JEPA latent prediction diffusion transformer LIBERO-Plus

简要说明：本文提出SaiVLA-JEPA，一种基于JEPA条件的VLA策略，在LIBERO-Plus中评估未来潜变量预测对闭环控制的影响，发现JEPA特征提升成功率但未来监督无聚合增益，并提出了上下文重试和参数自适应变体。

TLDR: This paper introduces SaiVLA-JEPA, a JEPA-conditioned VLA policy that evaluates the impact of future latent prediction on closed-loop control in LIBERO-Plus, finding improved success from JEPA features but no aggregate gain from future supervision, and proposing context-retry and parameter-adaptation variants.

Abstract: Joint-embedding predictive architectures (JEPAs) learn to predict representations rather than pixels, offering a compact way to model how visual scenes evolve. We ask whether this predictive objective improves closed-loop control when a vision-language-action (VLA) policy already uses V-JEPA features. We call our JEPA-conditioned flow policy family SaiVLA-JEPA. It combines a vision-language model with a diffusion transformer action expert and receives features from a frozen V-JEPA encoder. Two lightweight auxiliary heads predict future representations during training and are omitted at inference, leaving the action sampler unchanged. We compare policies without JEPA features, with current JEPA conditioning, and with additional future supervision on 10,030 paired LIBERO-Plus episodes. Current JEPA conditioning reaches about 82.5% success versus 71.6% without JEPA, while future supervision provides no detectable aggregate gain at the evaluated training budget. We separately evaluate SaiVLA-JEPA-TCA, which adapts latent context, and SaiVLA-JEPA-TPA, which adapts selected parameters. The context-retry gain is a system-level result under a larger attempt budget; parameter adaptation shows no consistent advantage over the frozen policy and target controls. These results motivate assessing latent-prediction objectives through paired closed-loop rollouts rather than latent loss alone.

Skeleton-Guided Geometry-Aware Auto-Labeling for Robotic Grasping: From 4-DoF Learning to 6-DoF Execution Manipulation

auto-labeling robotic grasping skeleton-based 4-DoF 6-DoF

简要说明：本文提出一种基于骨架的自动标注流程，用于机器人抓取，可自动生成4自由度与6自由度的抓取标注。文中提出SkelGG-CNN用于4自由度抓取，并开发了广义准3D骨架化方法以支持6自由度抓取，在仿真和真实实验中均取得了高成功率。

TLDR: This paper introduces a skeleton-based auto-labeling pipeline for robotic grasping, enabling automatic generation of 4-DoF and 6-DoF grasp annotations. It proposes SkelGG-CNN for 4-DoF grasping and a generalized quasi-3D skeletonization for 6-DoF, achieving high success rates in simulation and real-world experiments.

Abstract: Robotic grasping relies on large manually annotated datasets, which are costly to create. We introduce a straight-skeleton-based algorithm for generating two-finger grasp keypoints and identifying regions suitable for stable, feasible grasps. Our automated pipeline produces consistent grasp annotations without human intervention. We also propose the Skeleton-based Generative Grasp Convolutional Neural Network (SkelGG-CNN), a 4-DoF architecture that learns geometric grasp features from skeleton-based guidance. To extend auto-labeling to 6-DoF grasping, we develop a generalized quasi-3D skeletonization algorithm that operates directly on point clouds. Using automatically generated 4-DoF labels, SkelGG-CNN achieves accuracies of 95.42%, 96.55%, and 97.1% on Jacquard V1, Jacquard V2, and Cornell, respectively. On the GraspNet novel split, 6-DoF auto-labeling achieves an average precision of 18.33, comparable to state-of-the-art model-based approaches. In simulation, the 6-DoF method achieves success rates of 98.4% on Dex-Net and 89.1% on EGAD!. Real-world experiments with a Delta parallel robot show success rates of 97.4% on single household objects and 95.0% in cluttered scenes, outperforming state-of-the-art methods without domain adaptation. The 4-DoF pipeline adapts across environments and produces annotations approaching human-level precision, enabling efficient training of lightweight models on small datasets. The generalized 6-DoF method provides scalable, geometry-aware annotations for cluttered 3D scenes.

ELEVATE: Agentic Controller Acquisition from Task Failure for Quadrupedal Manipulation Locomotion

quadrupedal manipulation whole-body control reinforcement learning task failure capability library

简要说明：ELEVATE是一个框架，通过利用任务失败触发编码智能体自动制定和优化强化学习问题，使四足操作机器人能够自主获取新的全身控制能力，并在仿真任务和实体机器人迁移中验证了该方法的有效性。

TLDR: ELEVATE is a framework that enables a quadrupedal manipulator to autonomously acquire new whole-body control capabilities by using task failures to trigger a coding agent to formulate and refine RL problems, and it validates the approach across simulation tasks and physical robot transfer.

Abstract: Quadrupedal manipulators can extend their workspace through whole-body interaction, but task-planning agents' capabilities remain limited by their available controllers. We present ELEVATE, a framework that uses persistent failures during task-program refinement to identify and acquire missing whole-body control capabilities. When these failures suggest a missing physical capability, a coding agent formulates and iteratively refines a reinforcement-learning problem, specifying behavior-specific rewards, curricula, and termination conditions. Validated controllers are added to the capability library and composed with existing controllers to retry the task. Starting from a single pretrained loco-manipulation controller, ELEVATE autonomously acquires controllers for wall-supported elevation, supported manipulation, and wall descent across a sequence of seven simulation tasks. These controllers are reused in subsequent reaching and object-manipulation tasks. On the five tasks beyond the initial controller's workspace, ELEVATE achieves mean success rates of 98.4%-100% across five independent runs, while coding-agent baselines with a fixed controller library fail to complete these tasks. The synthesized task programs, together with the acquired controllers, also transfer to a physical quadrupedal manipulator without real-robot training or fine-tuning, achieving 18/20 successes in rack retrieval and 16/20 in floor-to-rack placement. Videos and demonstrations are available at https://agentic-quad-elevate.github.io/public/.

CSO: Refining Robotic Policies via Skill Distribution Alignment and Skill-Grained Optimization Manipulation

简要说明：本文提出了CSO，一种两阶段的机器人策略后训练框架，通过拒绝采样监督微调和技能级策略优化，在LIBERO、RoboTwin2.0和真实世界任务中提升了成功率。

TLDR: This paper introduces CSO, a two-stage post-training framework for refining discrete-skill robotic policies. It uses rejection-sampling supervised fine-tuning and a skill-level policy optimization to improve success rates on LIBERO, RoboTwin2.0, and real-world tasks.

Abstract: Discretizing continuous actions into discrete skills using methods like VQ-VAE has emerged as a powerful paradigm for robotic manipulation. However, quantization errors yield a suboptimal distribution for the skill prior, degrading its performance. While reinforcement learning offers a path for refinement, its direct application faces unstable encoder updates and a granularity dilemma in importance sampling. To address these challenges, we introduce Cascaded Skills Optimization (CSO), a two-stage post-training framework. First, Rejection-Sampling Supervised Fine-tuning aligns the model's observation-to-skill mapping with the distribution of successful online trajectories via supervised fine-tuning. Second, with the observation encoder frozen, Skills Policy Optimization computes an independent, clipped importance ratio for each complete skill, enabling stable and efficient updates. With a lightweight QueST-style policy, CSO raises overall LIBERO success from 80.1% to 97.5%, improves performance on RoboTwin2.0, and achieves 61.0% success across five physical single-arm tasks. Additional comparisons with FAST, discrete BEAST, and fixed-buffer IQL further validate the effectiveness of our two-stage post-training strategy.

Proprioceptive Finger Force Estimation for Reliable Box Manipulation Manipulation

简要说明：本文提出FEEL，一种基于本体感觉信号的指力估计方法，通过不确定性感知的残差学习提高箱体抓取放置的可靠性和无损成功率，无需专用接触传感器。

TLDR: This paper introduces FEEL, an uncertainty-aware residual method for estimating per-finger external forces from proprioceptive signals, which improves the reliability and damage-free outcomes of box pick-and-place on a real robot without dedicated contact sensors.

Abstract: Reliable box manipulation requires successful placement without damage, which depends on maintaining sufficient contact force without excessive loading. We hypothesize that per-finger force feedback improves manipulation reliability relative to open-loop finger commands. To this end, we present Finger External-Force Estimation from Proprioceptive SignaLs (FEEL), an uncertainty-aware residual method that estimates per-finger external force magnitude from proprioceptive histories. Our method combines a calibrated virtual-spring estimate with a residual distribution predicted by a Mixture Density Network (MDN) and requires no dedicated contact sensors during deployment. The estimated force is used for continuous per-finger adaptation, while predictive uncertainty is evaluated as an empirical signal of ambiguity in proprioceptive force estimation. Across ten fingers and contact forces spanning 0-20 N, our method achieves a mean RMSE of 0.251 N, reducing the error by 92.8% relative to the virtual spring and by 9.4% relative to an end-to-end MDN. In box pick-and-place, force adaptation maintains task success at 10/10 and increases both damage-free outcomes and reliability from 7/10 to 10/10. The project page is available at https://finger-force-estimation.pages.dev/

What Should a Navigator Remember? Spatiotemporal Memory for Zero-Shot Vision-Language Navigation VLN

zero-shot VLN spatiotemporal memory navigation robot Qwen3

简要说明：本文提出一种零样本视觉语言导航系统，通过结构化时空记忆（任务进度、观测历史、几何地图）结合8B观察者与推理者，在R2R-CE及真实机器人实验中表现优异。

TLDR: This paper introduces a zero-shot Vision-Language Navigation (VLN) system that uses structured spatiotemporal memory (task progress, observation history, and geometric map) with an 8B observer and reasoner, demonstrating strong performance on R2R-CE and real-world robot experiments.

Abstract: Zero-shot vision-and-language navigation (VLN) requires an agent to use past observations without navigation-specific training. We study what information an 8B-scale navigation system should retain and how it should be organized for subsequent decisions. Our agent combines instruction progress, hierarchically compressed observation history anchored to poses, and accumulated geometry in complementary task, temporal, and spatial memories. An 8B observer summarizes observations, and an 8B reasoner uses the memories to select waypoint intents or request additional views, a geometric controller executes the selected targets. A semantic information perspective motivates the distinction between retaining evidence, making it accessible to the reasoner, and acquiring further observations. On R2R-CE val-unseen, the complete system achieves the highest among the evaluated Qwen3-based zero-shot baselines. Removing temporal memory reduces SR and sharply increases average path length from 16.9 to 39.1m, removing the spatial-map and planning component reduces SR as well. Real-world experiments on a Unitree Go2 show that our zero-shot method achieves higher success rates than the post-trained baseline in both corridor and office environments, suggesting better transfer to the evaluated real-world settings. As a complementary evaluation, LAW2 uses paired instruction interventions to examine behavioral responses, instruction following ability beyond endpoint success. These results support preserving task progress, observation history, and geometry in structured memory for zero-shot navigation with smaller open models.

MATCH: Execution-Aware Hierarchical Reinforcement Learning for Vision-and-Language Navigation in Continuous Environment VLN

Vision-and-Language Navigation Hierarchical Reinforcement Learning Continuous Environment Execution-Aware Planning Embodied Agent

简要说明：MATCH 通过分层强化学习联合训练语言条件规划器和目标条件执行器，利用执行感知拓扑图与分阶段课程，在连续环境视觉-语言导航（VLN-CE）的 R2R-CE 和 RxR-CE 基准上达到最先进性能，并展示了真实机器人部署潜力。

TLDR: MATCH jointly learns a language-conditioned planner and a goal-conditioned executor in hierarchical RL for Vision-and-Language Navigation in continuous environments, using an execution-aware topological graph and staged curriculum to achieve state-of-the-art results on R2R-CE and RxR-CE benchmarks with real-robot deployment potential.

Abstract: Vision-and-Language Navigation in Continuous Environments (VLN-CE) requires instruction-consistent planning and reliable execution under collision constraints. Yet many hierarchical approaches optimize the planner while keeping execution fixed. We introduce MATCH, a framework that jointly learns a language-conditioned planner and a goal-conditioned primitive-action executor. An execution-aware topological graph represents start-dependent execution options and integrates executor value estimates with realized outcome memory, enabling the planner to select execution requests and refine their targets through bounded continuous offsets. A transition-aware dense macro reward balances ordered progress along the reference route against execution residual, encouraging executable requests without substituting local success for instruction following.
