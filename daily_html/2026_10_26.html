RynnVLA-Latent: Scaling Robotic Manipulation Foundation Models with 3D-Grounded Latent Actions VLA

latent actions 3D-grounded flow matching pretraining manipulation

简要说明：RynnVLA-Latent通过从异构视频中学习3D基础潜在动作来扩展机器人操作基础模型，在仿真和真实世界操作基准上取得了最先进的结果。

TLDR: RynnVLA-Latent scales robotic manipulation foundation models by learning 3D-grounded latent actions from heterogeneous videos, achieving state-of-the-art results across simulation and real-world manipulation benchmarks.

Abstract: Scaling vision-language-action (VLA) models is constrained by scarce action-labeled demonstrations and incompatible control spaces across robot embodiments. We present RynnVLA-Latent, a framework that turns heterogeneous interaction videos into scalable supervision for robot policy pretraining. At its core, RynnLAM learns continuous, 3D-grounded latent actions from visual transitions without requiring native robot controls. By combining residual future-feature prediction, camera-compensated 3D-flow supervision, and camera-motion decoupling, RynnLAM encourages representations that capture control-relevant motion rather than appearance changes alone. We apply RynnLAM to 30.1K hours of human, robot, and simulation videos, producing 745.52M latent-action descriptors. RynnVLA-Latent then learns to predict latent-action chunks from visual observations and language through flow matching, before adapting its pretrained backbone and action expert to embodiment-specific robot actions. The resulting 4B policy achieves 98.8% success on LIBERO, 84.7% on LIBERO-Plus without benchmark-specific adaptation, 51.9% average success across five VLABench tracks, and 88.6% average success on the RoboTwin 2.0 bimanual benchmark. It also achieves the highest observed success rates among the compared methods on six real-world manipulation tasks across dexterous-hand and mobile-gripper platforms. Together with representation ablations and data-scaling results, these findings demonstrate the potential of latent-action supervision for scaling robot policy learning beyond native action-labeled data.

RoboDuo: Attempt, Check and Atomic Recovery through Code for Vision-Language-Action Models VLA

Vision-Language-Action model closed-loop framework atomic recovery long-horizon tasks code as interface

简要说明：RoboDuo 是一个闭环框架，结合编码代理和两个 VLA 来检查任务完成情况并利用原子技能从失败中恢复，在不更新权重的情况下提升 VLA 在长时程任务上的表现。

TLDR: RoboDuo is a closed-loop framework combining a coding agent with two VLAs to check task completion and recover from failures using atomic skills, improving VLA performance on long-horizon tasks without weight updates.

Abstract: Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models map a language instruction and the current observation directly to actions, without checking whether the instruction has been completed. For long-horizon tasks, a single failed step therefore goes unnoticed and leaves the whole task unfinished. Such failed episodes, however, often end with the task partially complete. Moreover, the unfinished steps are reusable atomic skills that recur across demonstrations. A failed attempt can thus be recovered by composing with atomic skills. We propose RoboDuo, a closed-loop dual-system framework that uses code as the interface between a coding agent and two VLAs. The agent writes a program that lets a full-task VLA attempt the whole task and checks whether it is complete; on failure, an atomic VLA finishes the remaining steps. Since the agent emits no actions and only decides which VLA for execution, RoboDuo wraps an existing full-task VLA without updating its weights. To train the atomic VLA and the coding agent with the completion-checking ability, we build LIBERO-Atomic, LIBERO-Code, and LIBERO-Judge from a single set of LIBERO demonstrations. RoboDuo consistently improves X-VLA and $π_{0.5}$ on LIBERO and LIBERO-PRO, with the largest gains on long-horizon tasks, and recovers failed rollouts of a frozen full-task VLA on a real robot.

IntNet: A Communication-Driven Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning Framework for Cooperative Autonomous Driving AutonomousDriving

Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning Cooperative Autonomous Driving Communication Scheduling Intent Sharing Vehicle-to-Vehicle Network

简要说明：IntNet 是一个用于协作式自动驾驶的多智能体强化学习框架，通过共享预测意图和自适应通信调度来提升安全性并降低带宽消耗。

TLDR: IntNet is a multi-agent reinforcement learning framework for cooperative autonomous driving that shares predicted intents and adaptively schedules communication, improving safety and reducing bandwidth usage.

Abstract: Achieving safety in autonomous driving through Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning (MARL) is a critical yet challenging task due to non-stationarity, partial observability, and the need for effective coordination among agents. Although earlier cooperative MARL methods have aimed to improve coordination by sharing encoded state observations, we find these strategies are insufficient in safety-critical scenarios. To address this gap, we present IntNet, a novel MARL framework that enhances safety by incorporating the transmission of vehicle intent and adaptive communication scheduling into a unified end-to-end learning paradigm, jointly optimising all components for safe coordination. Central to our approach is an observation prediction module that enables agents to forecast subsequent policy outputs, which they can then share across a vehicle-to-vehicle network. Our model-free architecture employs a Graph Attention Network to process incoming messages and a scheduler component to dynamically optimise the communication graph for bandwidth efficiency. We compare IntNet against state-of-the-art communicative MARL methods in complex urban environments with both autonomous and human-operated vehicles, achieving improved coordination, lower collision rates, and reducing communication efforts by up to 60\%. Through extensive experiments, we assess the framework's learning efficiency, scalability, and the balance between information sharing and bandwidth usage for collision-free trajectories.

Scaling Dexterous Manipulation via Action Representation Pretraining Manipulation

简要说明：本文提出了ARPG，在大规模人类操作数据上预训练动作表示编码器并冻结，同时训练紧凑的生成模型进行动作生成，在双手灵巧操作基准上取得了最优性能。

TLDR: This paper introduces ARPG, which pretrains an action representation encoder on large-scale human manipulation data and freezes it while training a compact generative model for action generation, achieving state-of-the-art performance on bimanual dexterous manipulation benchmarks.

Abstract: Large-scale vision, language, and video pretraining has substantially improved the perception and generalization capabilities of robotic manipulation models, but action generation for complex dexterous manipulation remains constrained by the scarcity of robot demonstrations. Limited robot demonstrations must be used to learn both action representations and task-conditioned action distributions, making it difficult to adequately learn action representations and, in turn, limiting the ability of downstream generative models to generate complex dexterous actions. To address this limitation, we propose ARPG (Action Representation Pretraining and Generation), which explicitly separates action representation learning from action generation: we first pretrain an action representation encoder on large-scale human manipulation data. We then freeze the encoder and train a compact generative backbone (130M parameters) to learn task-conditioned action distributions in the encoder's representation space. Experimental results show that ARPG achieves state-of-the-art performance on the DexJoCo benchmark and improves the average success rate on bimanual dexterous manipulation from 10.1% for $π_{0.5}$ to 44.7%. Scaling experiments further show that broader coverage of human manipulation behaviors effectively improves downstream performance. These results indicate that action representation pretraining provides an independent scaling dimension for improving complex dexterous manipulation capabilities.

TLDR: ThinkLocoManip presents an agentic harness for legged loco-manipulation that interleaves reasoning and acting, integrating perception, navigation, manipulation, and locomotion tools, achieving a 77% success rate in long-horizon real-world tasks.

Abstract: In this paper, we propose ThinkLocoManip, an agentic harness for legged loco-manipulation designed to tackle complex, long-horizon tasks in large-scale environments. Existing approaches typically follow either direct action generation or a plan-then-act paradigm, both of which can struggle with the generalization and error-recovery capabilities required for extended task execution. In contrast, ThinkLocoManip adopts an interleaved reasoning-and-acting paradigm, allowing the agent to continuously reason about its current state, execute actions, observe their outcomes, and adapt its strategy accordingly. Building on this paradigm, our agentic harness orchestrates a suite of specialized tools for perception, navigation, manipulation, and locomotion, enabling flexible composition of heterogeneous capabilities. Unlike existing robotic agentic harnesses, ThinkLocoManip explicitly incorporates locomotion as a tool domain for agent reasoning and execution. We further design a set of skills that explicitly account for both tool usage within each functional domain and coordination across domains, supporting effective execution of complex behaviors over long horizons. To tackle task failure during long-horizon interactions, we develop keyframe thinking mechanism to preserve critical execution context and support efficient error recovery. Extensive real-world experiments demonstrate that ThinkLocoManip substantially outperforms representative methods based on both direct action generation and plan-then-act paradigms across challenging long-horizon legged loco-manipulation tasks. Notably, ThinkLocoManip achieves a success rate of 77%, more than twice that of the strongest plan-then-act baseline (37.1%). Our experiments further validate the effectiveness of interleaved reasoning and acting, tool orchestration, and coordinated skill execution.

AdaPolicy: Action-Driven Anticipatory Robot Policies via Next-Chunk Prediction Manipulation

简要说明：AdaPolicy 提出了一种轻量级的动作驱动预测策略，通过级联下一块预测将隐式预见性注入机器人策略，无需未来观测编码，在基准测试和真实任务中均取得一致提升。

TLDR: AdaPolicy introduces a lightweight action-driven anticipatory policy that uses cascaded next-chunk prediction to instill implicit foresight into robot policies without future-observation encoding, achieving consistent gains on benchmarks and real-world tasks.

Abstract: Endowing robot policies with anticipatory capabilities has proven effective in improving control performance. However, existing methods predominantly rely on explicit supervision derived from future observations, in either pixel or latent space, at the cost of additional architectural and computational complexity. Departing from explicit future supervision, we ask whether such foresight can be elicited directly from the action signal. To this end, we propose \textbf{AdaPolicy}, an \textbf{A}ction-\textbf{d}riven \textbf{a}nticipatory policy framework that shapes future-aware representations via cascaded next-chunk prediction. Specifically, an Online Action Head jointly produces the current action chunk and an evolved latent state. Building on this state, a cascaded Unrolled Action Head forecasts the next chunk, and the resulting loss flows back through the evolved state to instill implicit foresight into the online policy. Importantly, this training-only unroll requires no future-observation encoding, keeping AdaPolicy lightweight to train with zero inference-time overhead. Experiments on MetaWorld, RoboTwin 2.0, LIBERO, and real-world deployments demonstrate consistent gains across diverse settings, including up to $14$ percentage points on millimeter-precision tasks such as earphone assembly and shoelace threading. More details and visualizations are available at \href{https://teee111.github.io/AdaPolicy/}{https://teee111.github.io/AdaPolicy/}.

HuGe: Scaling Humanoid Generalist Vision-Language-Action Models with Egocentric Experience HumanoidEmbodied

Humanoid Generalist VLA egocentric demonstrations whole-body control three-stage training pseudo-proprioceptive states

简要说明：HuGe在超过2000小时的自我中心人类和机器人数据上预训练人形通用VLA模型，通过三阶段训练流程（包括伪本体感觉和视觉适配）实现跨实体迁移，在HumanoidArena基准和Unitree G1真实世界任务中达到领先水平。

TLDR: HuGe pre-trains humanoid generalist VLAs on 2,000 hours of egocentric human and robot data, using a three-stage pipeline with pseudo-proprioception and visual adaptation to transfer across embodiments, achieving state-of-the-art results on HumanoidArena and real-world Unitree G1 pick-and-drop tasks.

Abstract: Extending Vision-Language-Action models (VLAs) to humanoid robots requires coordinated whole-body control and diverse demonstrations that are costly to collect in real-world scenarios. Although egocentric human demonstrations provide a complementary source of task experience, differences in visual embodiment and missing robot proprioception complicate transfer to humanoid policies. To address these challenges, we introduce HuGe, a family of Humanoid Generalist VLAs pre-trained on over 2,000 hours of egocentric human and robot data. Specifically, HuGe employs three existing representative VLA architectures to predict latent motion tokens for a pretrained whole-body controller, together with hand-control signals. Based on this, we introduce a three-stage training pipeline that combines egocentric pre-training, humanoid adaptation, and downstream fine-tuning. To facilitate transfer across embodiments, we construct pseudo-proprioceptive states through simulation playback during pre-training and modify arm appearance in egocentric observations through re-rendering during downstream fine-tuning.
