PGX: Physically Grounded Execution for Long-Horizon Humanoid Loco-Manipulation HumanoidEmbodied

vision-language-action (VLA) models long-horizon loco-manipulation predicate-based state machine world-coordinate locking humanoid robot

简要说明：PGX 将长时程人形机器人移动操作任务分解为基于谓词的状态机、VLA 技能和世界坐标锁定三部分，在仿真和真实 Unitree G1 机器人上提升了任务成功率。

TLDR: PGX decomposes long-horizon humanoid loco-manipulation into a predicate-based state machine, VLA skills, and world-coordinate locking, improving success rates on simulated and real tasks with a Unitree G1 robot.

Abstract: Robotic vision-language-action (VLA) models can execute diverse individual manipulation skills, yet they still struggle with long-horizon loco-manipulation tasks, where temporal structure, skills, and precision are tightly coupled within a monolithic model. We propose Physically Grounded Execution (PGX), a framework that offloads temporal and precision control to two classical training-free components, allowing the VLA to focus on stage-specific skill dynamics. PGX decomposes long-horizon loco-manipulation into three layers: (i) a predicate-based state machine governs task progression, using physical predicates to gate phase transitions, trigger recovery, and coordinate navigation and waiting. Shared predicate semantics connect offline demonstration segmentation, online execution, and stage-wise evaluation; (ii) VLA skills operate within active manipulation phases, with flat or stage-trained models concentrating on task-relevant actions; and (iii) world-coordinate locking (WCL) maintains spatial consistency through base station keeping, object-relative positioning, and world-anchored contact targets. We demonstrate the effectiveness of PGX with extensive experiments conducted on three simulated tasks and one real-world task using a Unitree G1 humanoid robot for long-horizon loco-manipulation, showing improvements in stage progression and task success rates. Project page is available at: https://kl-research.github.io/pgx

One Prior, Two Dynamics: Repurposing Video Diffusion Transformers for Self-Supervised Latent Action Learning Manipulation

latent action learning video diffusion transformer inverse dynamics forward dynamics self-supervised

简要说明：O2LaF 重新利用预训练的视频扩散变换器，同时学习逆动态和正动态，从未标记视频中发现可迁移的潜在动作，在导航和机器人操作中实现少样本动作探针和零样本动作迁移。

TLDR: O2LaF repurposes a pretrained video diffusion transformer to simultaneously learn inverse and forward dynamics, discovering transferable latent actions from unlabeled videos for few-shot action probing and zero-shot action transfer in navigation and robotic manipulation.

Abstract: Videos reveal how the world changes, but rarely the actions that cause those changes. Latent action learning recovers such supervision from visual transitions, yet often learns the underlying dynamics largely from scratch. We ask whether a pretrained video generator can instead provide the prior for both inverse and forward dynamics. We introduce O$^{2}$LaF, built on a simple principle: one prior, two dynamics. We initialize both models from the same pretrained video DiT and adapt them to their distinct roles: a feed-forward transition encoder with learnable action queries for inverse dynamics, and a causal, action-conditioned video generator for forward dynamics. By controlling information access and optimizing both dynamics end-to-end through a compact bottleneck, O$^{2}$LaF learns transferable actions from unlabeled videos. Across navigation and robotic manipulation, O$^{2}$LaF sets a new state of the art in few-shot action probing and enables stronger zero-shot action transfer across visual contexts and embodiments. Beyond generative video priors, we further extend our framework to predictive representation priors such as V-JEPA2, achieving competitive performance. Our findings suggest a broader paradigm for latent-action learning: repurposing pretrained video models to discover transferable actions and predict their consequences.

AgentRoboCritic: Understanding the Capability of Frontier Agents on Direct Robot Control Manipulation

action-level analysis direct robot control critique framework frontier multimodal agents manipulation benchmarks

简要说明：本文提出了动作级分析与评判框架AgentRoboCritic，用于评估前沿多模态代理在12个操作基准上的直接机器人控制能力，揭示了其优势与局限，并推出了一个微调的开源评判模型以支持低成本分析。

TLDR: This paper presents an action-level analysis and critique framework, AgentRoboCritic, to evaluate frontier multimodal agents on direct robot control across 12 manipulation benchmarks, revealing strengths and limitations, and introduces a fine-tuned open-source critic for low-cost analysis.

Abstract: Frontier multimodal language agents such as GPT-6 Astra have demonstrated strong capabilities in controlling robots directly via low-level numerical actions without high-level primitives. However, existing benchmarks report only episode-level success rates, which show neither how these agents succeed nor where they fail. In this paper, we present a systematic action-level analysis of frontier agents on direct robot control across 12 manipulation benchmarks. We introduce AgentRoboCritic, an intent-grounded critique framework in which the policy agent states a local intent alongside each action, and a critic evaluates the action's outcome and failure mode against this intent. Our action-level analysis demonstrates the promising capabilities of frontier agents on direct robot control, including closed-loop recovery from local execution failures, reliable semantic grounding across diverse scenes, and strong zero-shot manipulation without task-specific adaptation. It also identifies their current limitations, including task-relevant physical execution bottlenecks such as grasping and alignment, and uneven recovery across failure modes. Finally, we validate our critic on a 400-action human-annotated benchmark and release a dataset of 12,844 annotated actions alongside a fine-tuned open-source critic, Muse-Glimmer-30B, whose failure-mode accuracy rises from 32.58% to 74.24%, enabling low-cost action-level analysis.

摘要（中文）：前沿多模态语言代理（如GPT-6 Astra）已展现出无需高级原语、直接通过低级数值动作控制机器人的强大能力。然而，现有基准仅报告回合级成功率，既无法揭示这些代理如何成功，也无法揭示其在何处失败。本文对前沿代理在12个操作基准上的直接机器人控制进行了系统的动作级分析。我们提出AgentRoboCritic，一种基于意图的评判框架，其中策略代理在每次动作时附带陈述局部意图，评判器则依据该意图评估动作结果及失败模式。我们的动作级分析展示了前沿代理在直接机器人控制方面的潜力能力，包括对局部执行失败的闭环恢复、跨多样场景的可靠语义 grounding，以及在无需任务特定适配情况下的强大零样本操作能力。同时，分析也揭示了它们当前的局限性，包括任务相关的物理执行瓶颈（如抓取和对准）以及各失败模式间恢复能力的不均衡。最后，我们在包含400个动作的人工标注基准上验证了评判器，并发布了一个包含12,844个标注动作的数据集以及微调后的开源评判器Muse-Glimmer-30B，其失败模式准确率从32.58%提升至74.24%，从而实现了低成本的动作级分析。

PhyDEX: A Benchmark for Physical Reasoning in Vision-Language-Action Models via Dynamic Extrinsic Dexterity VLA

throwing benchmark dynamic extrinsic dexterity physical reasoning vision-language-action models mass and friction inference

简要说明：提出了PhyDEX，一个用于视觉-语言-动作模型的投掷基准，要求推断物理属性（质量、质心、摩擦）以执行动态外部灵巧性，揭示了当前VLA策略的成功率低于3%且存在物理盲区。

TLDR: Introduces PhyDEX, a throwing benchmark for vision-language-action models that requires inferring physical properties (mass, center of mass, friction) to perform dynamic extrinsic dexterity, revealing that current VLA policies achieve <3% success and are physically blind.

Abstract: Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models have advanced rapidly in language-conditioned robotic manipulation, yet existing benchmarks mainly evaluate gripper--object contact and largely ignore how objects interact with the environment through gravity, friction, inertia, and mass distribution. As a result, it remains unclear whether current policies truly understand physics or merely interpolate from their training data. We address this gap through the lens of dynamic extrinsic dexterity and introduce PhyDEX, a throwing benchmark in which a robot must infer an object's mass, center of mass, and friction to send it to a destination beyond its kinematic workspace. PhyDEX contains 35K trajectories (143 hours) across nine tasks and 100 scenes, covering uniform, irregular, and dynamic-mass objects with static, multiple, and moving destinations. All data are generated by a scalable, fully automatic pipeline that combines grasp generation, motion planning, and closed-loop throw optimization under randomized physical properties. We evaluate three representative VLAs, GR00T N1.7, DynamicVLA, and ForceVLA, and find that all achieve success rates below 3\%. Our analysis reveals two key bottlenecks: limited robustness of pretrained models, which causes most failures before the throw, and physical blindness, as providing ground-truth physical properties roughly doubles success. Experiments on a humanoid robot and in the real world show the same failure patterns. We hope PhyDEX will drive the development of physically grounded robot policies.

摘要（中文）：视觉-语言-行动（VLA）模型在语言条件下的机器人操作领域取得了迅速进展，然而现有基准主要评估夹爪与物体的接触，在很大程度上忽略了物体如何通过重力、摩擦力、惯性和质量分布与环境相互作用。因此，当前策略究竟是真正理解物理，还是仅从训练数据中插值，仍然不得而知。我们通过动态外在灵巧性的视角来弥补这一空白，并提出了PhyDEX——一个投掷基准，要求机器人推断物体的质量、质心和摩擦力，以将其送到其运动学工作空间之外的目的地。PhyDEX包含35,000条轨迹（143小时），涵盖九个任务和100个场景，覆盖均匀物体、不规则物体和动态质量物体，并涉及静态、多个和移动目的地。所有数据均由一个可扩展的全自动流水线生成，该流水线结合了抓取生成、运动规划以及随机物理属性下的闭环投掷优化。我们评估了三个具有代表性的VLA模型——GR00T N1.7、DynamicVLA和ForceVLA，发现它们的成功率均低于3%。我们的分析揭示了两个关键瓶颈：预训练模型鲁棒性有限，导致大多数失败发生在投掷之前；以及物理盲区，因为提供真实物理属性会使成功率大约翻倍。在人形机器人上和真实世界中的实验显示出相同的失败模式。我们希望PhyDEX能够推动具有物理基础的机器人策略的发展。

ScaleWorld: High-Resolution World Generation through Shared Prediction and Refinement WorldModel

video world model high-resolution generation causal model shared backbone task-asymmetric distillation

简要说明：ScaleWorld提出了一种因果视频世界模型，在单一共享骨干中统一低分辨率预测与高分辨率细化，实现高效的高分辨率视频生成。

TLDR: ScaleWorld introduces a causal video world model that unifies low-resolution prediction and high-resolution refinement in a single shared backbone, achieving efficient high-resolution video generation for world modeling.

Abstract: High-resolution generation enables video world models to render fine-grained scene structures and textures, but directly generating high-resolution video incurs substantial computational and memory costs. We present ScaleWorld, a causal video model that unifies low-resolution world prediction and high-resolution refinement within a single shared backbone. By refining its own predictions, ScaleWorld produces high-resolution world videos without requiring a separate refinement model. For each video chunk, the shared backbone first completes a low-resolution prediction and then performs one-step high-resolution refinement conditioned on that prediction. To support efficient streaming generation, we maintain separate causal states with a long low-resolution history for prediction and a short high-resolution history for refinement. To address task interference during distillation, we separate specialist optimization from capability consolidation and introduce task-asymmetric on-policy distillation (TA-OPD). This procedure transfers prediction and refinement behaviors from specialized teachers into a shared student through local velocity matching under their respective sampling and history policies. {Extensive experiments demonstrate that ScaleWorld achieves high-quality world generation with improved computational and memory efficiency.

摘要（中文）：高分辨率生成使视频世界模型能够渲染细粒度的场景结构和纹理，但直接生成高分辨率视频会带来巨大的计算和内存开销。我们提出ScaleWorld，一种因果视频模型，在单一共享骨干网络内统一了低分辨率世界预测与高分辨率细化。通过细化自身的预测结果，ScaleWorld无需单独的细化模型即可生成高分辨率世界视频。对于每个视频块，共享骨干网络首先完成低分辨率预测，然后基于该预测执行一步高分辨率细化。为支持高效的流式生成，我们维护独立的因果状态：使用长低分辨率历史进行预测，并使用短高分辨率历史进行细化。为解决蒸馏过程中的任务干扰问题，我们将专家优化与能力整合分离，并引入任务非对称策略蒸馏（TA-OPD）。该过程通过在各自采样和历史策略下的局部速度匹配，将预测与细化行为从专用教师模型迁移到共享学生模型中。大量实验表明，ScaleWorld在实现高质量世界生成的同时，显著提升了计算和内存效率。

A Tutorial on Complementarity-Based Contact Models for Robotics Other

complementarity contact models rigid-body dynamics frictional contact simulation

简要说明：本教程为机器人学中的互补性接触模型提供了自成体系的教学性论述，将刚体动力学、锥优化和摩擦接触联系起来，并比较了不同公式和求解器。

TLDR: This tutorial provides a self-contained pedagogical treatment of complementarity-based contact models for robotics, connecting rigid-body dynamics, conic optimization, and frictional contact, and comparing formulations and solvers.

Abstract: Simulating rigid-body dynamics with contact is fundamental to robotics, from motion synthesis and control to mechanism design. Many widely used simulators rely on cone complementarity formulations, whose physical assumptions and numerical solution require substantial background. While excellent surveys, monographs, and course notes exist, they mostly address computational mechanics or computer graphics or document a single simulator. To the best of our knowledge, a self-contained pedagogical treatment for robotics, bringing these formulations and their solvers together with complete derivations and a unified notation, does not yet exist. This tutorial addresses this gap by connecting rigid-body dynamics, conic optimization, and frictional contact. In particular, we explain how complementarity conditions encode non-penetration and frictional dissipation, determining separation, sticking, and sliding without prescribed contact modes. We distinguish equivalent transcriptions from convex relaxations, showing how the latter can introduce artifacts such as lift-off and creep. We examine the existence and uniqueness of motion and contact forces, including the solver's role in selecting among multiple solutions. We then compare first-order and Newton-based methods, explain regularization and proximal strategies, and discuss trade-offs between computational cost, robustness, and physical fidelity. Connections to widely used simulators, optimal control, and differentiable simulation help readers interpret simulation results, choose suitable formulations and solvers, and develop their own contact dynamics routines.
