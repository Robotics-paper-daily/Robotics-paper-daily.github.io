ForeAct: Geometry-Guided Action Learning from World Representations VLA

video world model geometry-guided action learning single-pass representation next-frame relative depth low-latency control

简要说明：ForeAct 将预训练的视频世界模型重新用作低延迟机器人控制的单次前向表示骨干，通过由下一帧相对深度监督的可学习标记来塑造共享视频表示以生成动作。在 LIBERO、SimplerEnv-Bridge 和真实世界 rollout 上取得了高成功率，且无需深度输入或外部几何骨干。

TLDR: ForeAct repurposes a pretrained video world model as a single-pass representation backbone for low-latency robotic control, using learnable tokens supervised by next-frame relative depth to shape the shared video representation for action generation. It achieves high success rates on LIBERO, SimplerEnv-Bridge, and real-world rollouts without needing depth input or an external geometry backbone.

Abstract: Robotic manipulation requires representations that capture both scene geometry and its evolution during interaction. Existing approaches have largely treated these two aspects separately. World action models capture such dynamics by coupling action prediction with future video generation, but their reliance on iterative diffusion or rectified-flow inference leads to high computational cost, while their representations lack explicit spatial structure. Explicitly modeling scene geometry typically requires auxiliary modules that increase both parameter count and computational cost. Conversely, geometry-centric policies primarily recover observed spatial structure and often lack explicit supervision for anticipating future geometric changes during interaction. To address these limitations, we introduce ForeAct, which repurposes a pretrained video world model as a single-pass representation backbone for low-latency control. ForeAct directly processes the clean latent of the current observation in one forward pass, eliminating future-frame synthesis and iterative denoising while retaining the backbone's pretrained spatiotemporal priors. Rather than introducing a separate geometry backbone, ForeAct uses learnable tokens supervised by next-frame relative depth to shape the shared video representation through bidirectional self-attention. This compact interface turns the spatial knowledge already encoded in the backbone into a future-aware representation for action generation, with little parameter overhead. The lightweight geometry readout is used only during training and removed at deployment, enabling low-latency, geometry-aware control without depth input or an external geometry backbone. Without additional large-scale pretraining, ForeAct achieves 96.6% average success on LIBERO, 63.5% on SimplerEnv-Bridge, and a 74.4% success rate across 90 real-world rollouts.

Zero-Shot VLA Adaptation with Retrieved Guidance VLA

Retrieval-Augmented Generation VLA adaptation Flow-matching Cross-embodiment Manipulation

简要说明：RAGu 提出了一种用于零样本 VLA 适应的检索增强生成框架，通过几何校准、粗到细检索和流匹配引导，在域偏移下提升操作成功率。

TLDR: RAGu introduces a retrieval-augmented generation framework for zero-shot VLA adaptation, using geometric calibration, coarse-to-fine retrieval, and flow-matching guidance to improve manipulation success under domain shifts.

Abstract: Pre-training large parametric "Vision-Language-Action" (VLA) models on large demonstration datasets has recently yielded promising results for general-purpose manipulation. However, these models still struggle under even small domain shifts relative to the training data, such as changes in camera viewpoint, background, object identity, or robot morphology. Motivated by the success of Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) in language models, we pursue retrieval to boost VLA performance. Specifically, we seek to leverage retrieval from domain-mismatched, "in-the-wild" demonstration datasets like DROID to guide a VLA's behavior in a new target domain. To achieve this, we introduce RAGu, which overcomes robotics-specific challenges for RAG: (1) It incorporates simple geometric calibration to transform source demonstrations into the target setup and reduce cross-domain differences. (2) It uses a coarse-to-fine retrieval strategy that selects, from a large pool of pre-warped candidates, the trajectory most compatible with the base VLA. (3) It guides the VLA's flow-matching inference procedure rather than seeking to retrain the base model or expand its limited context window. We evaluate RAGu across real-world, simulation, sim-to-real transfer, and cross-embodiment humanoid manipulation. We find it improves real-world VLA success rates from 20% to 80% and outperforms the closest-related baselines in sim. Project website: raguvla.github.io

Breaking survivorship bias in driving world models AutonomousDriving

简要说明：本文识别并解决了驾驶世界模型中的幸存者偏差，通过混合常规和事故视频训练中性世界模型，使其能更准确地预测碰撞并改善自动驾驶决策。

TLDR: This paper identifies and addresses survivorship bias in driving world models by training a neutral world model on a mixture of routine and accident videos, enabling more faithful collision anticipation and improved decision-making in autonomous driving.

Abstract: World models allow autonomous vehicles to imagine the consequences of candidate actions before execution, but their reliability depends on the futures represented in their training data. We identify a survivorship bias in driving world models: because the futures recorded in prevailing driving datasets almost always end safely, existing models often imagine benign outcomes even when a collision is imminent. We introduce a neutral world model (NWM) that learns both routine and safety-critical futures from a mixture of routine driving and real accident videos with a bounding-box-guided objective. The NWM more faithfully anticipates imminent collisions, while maintaining a low false collision rate and competitive generation quality in routine driving. We further use the model as an imagination engine for decision-making. Given multiple ego trajectories, the NWM predicts the future associated with each candidate, and a reward function evaluates these imagined outcomes before selecting an action. On challenging NAVSIM scenarios, this procedure selects the better trajectory more reliably than the baseline world model. Zero-shot experiments with real-world pedestrian dummy crossing data collected using an autonomous research vehicle further demonstrate transfer to unseen sensor observations. These results show that correcting survivorship bias enables world models to more faithfully reason about futures and support safety-critical decision-making in autonomous driving.

DexForesight: Grounding Dexterous Policies with Implicit Spatial Foresight Manipulation

dexterous manipulation world-action model implicit spatial foresight action-conditioned world model policy-world model alignment

简要说明：DexForesight通过将策略的潜在空间与在离线场景深度和运动学手部几何上训练的动作条件世界模型对齐，隐式地学习空间预见能力，提高了仿真和真实机器人上的灵巧操作成功率。

TLDR: DexForesight implicitly learns spatial foresight by aligning a policy's latent space with an action-conditioned world model trained on offline scene depth and kinematic hand geometry, improving dexterous manipulation success across simulation and real robots.

Abstract: Dexterous manipulation requires rich spatial foresight over both the surrounding scene and the robot hand. Existing world-action models often acquire such predictive knowledge through explicit future prediction. We introduce DexForesight, a framework that learns spatial foresight implicitly by injecting dynamics-aware world representations into the latent space of a dexterous policy. During training, DexForesight learns an action-conditioned spatial world model from offline scene depth, kinematic hand geometry, and demonstration trajectories. The world model predicts 3D hand-point motion and visual feature changes, capturing both articulated hand dynamics and scene changes under demonstrated actions, including the motion of fingers occluded from the cameras. We then freeze the world model and align policy queries with its predictive world queries, transferring the learned spatial dynamics into policy latents and using them to condition action generation. At deployment, only the policy is retained, operating on RGB observations, language, and robot state without depth input or explicit future prediction. On 11 DexJoCo simulation tasks, DexForesight improves the overall success rate of the base policy from 49.4% to 60.5%. On three real-robot dexterous manipulation tasks, DexForesight achieves 58.3% success, compared with 50.0% for the strongest baseline.Project page https://dexforesight.github.io/

MOSU2: Long-range Navigation System with Multi-modal Scene Understanding VLN

vision-language model long-range navigation trajectory selection scene understanding outdoor robot

简要说明：MOSU2 是一种远程导航系统，采用两阶段轨迹选择方法，第一阶段使用 VLM 进行情境推理，仅在需要时触发第二阶段获取几何和动态信息，在真实世界室外实验中提高了导航成功率和条件检测准确率。

TLDR: MOSU2 is a long-range navigation system that uses a two-stage trajectory selection approach, where first-stage contextual reasoning with VLMs triggers second-stage retrieval of geometric and dynamic information only when needed, improving navigation success and condition-detection accuracy in real-world outdoor experiments.

Abstract: Robots that travel long distances through public outdoor spaces must do more than avoid obstacles: they must remain on terrain suited to their mobility and decide when to proceed, yield, or detour around pedestrians, cyclists, vehicles, traffic signals, and temporary restrictions. These decisions are difficult because their correctness often depends on how a scene evolves, such as whether an approaching car is slowing or a cyclist will cross the robot's path. Vision-language models (VLMs) can interpret such cues, but they reason over images that lack metric geometry and motion. Supplying this evidence at every step is too slow for real-time navigation, and hand-crafted triggers cannot anticipate the open-ended variety of outdoor situations. We present MOSU2, a long-range navigation system with two-stage trajectory selection, in which first-stage contextual reasoning determines when additional evidence is needed and what to retrieve. Only when an interaction remains unresolved does the first stage trigger the second stage to retrieve geometric and dynamic information and align it with scene context to make navigation decisions. Building on MOSU, MOSU2 follows routes from GPS-based route planning and filters candidate trajectories through multi-modal traversability analysis before selecting a trajectory to move the robot. In real-world experiments spanning stop gestures, construction sites, crosswalks, and roads shared with vehicles, cyclists, and pedestrians, MOSU2 raises mean navigation success from 61.8% to 94.2% and condition-detection accuracy from 72.2% to 91.4% over its single-image predecessor, at about 2.2 times its mean decision latency. Project website: https://jinggm.github.io/mosu2/

CrossLiDAR: Controllable Multi-Sensor LiDAR Sequence Generation AutonomousDriving

LiDAR simulation multi-sensor diffusion transformer autonomous driving range images

简要说明：CrossLiDAR 提出了一个统一的基于 Transformer 的框架，用于可控的多传感器 LiDAR 序列生成，利用 rectified flow 目标、多模态扩散 Transformer 和 ControlNet 风格的条件化，在 nuScenes 和 Waymo 数据集上取得了最先进的性能。

TLDR: CrossLiDAR introduces a unified transformer-based framework for controllable, multi-sensor LiDAR sequence generation, using a rectified flow objective with Multi-Modal Diffusion Transformer and ControlNet-style conditioning to achieve state-of-the-art performance on nuScenes and Waymo.

Abstract: High-fidelity LiDAR simulation remains a bottleneck for scalable development and validation of autonomous systems. Existing generators can synthesize realistic scans, but they are typically constrained to a single sensor configuration, offer limited control over structure and geometry, and drift in long-horizon 4D generation. We present CrossLiDAR, a unified transformer-based framework capable of generating controllable and spatio-temporally consistent multi-sensor LiDAR sequences. Our approach leverages a rectified flow objective with a Multi-Modal Diffusion Transformer (MM-DiT) backbone, within a compact latent space of range images. To support multiple LiDAR sensors, an Inter-sensor Module enables interaction across sensors, while a ControlNet-style branch provides structural conditioning (e.g., BEV priors or 3D bounding boxes). For modeling the temporal consistency, CrossLiDAR generates short $N$-frame sequences and extends them autoregressively to improve long-horizon coherence. Our model significantly outperforms state-of-the-art baselines, achieving an average improvement of 29.56\% in JSD and 15.36\% in MMD evaluated on nuScenes and the Waymo Dataset. CrossLiDAR bridges the gap to modern autonomous-driving simulation by enabling calibration-consistent multi-sensor LiDAR rollouts over long horizons for controllable, scenario-based testing.

TLDR: This paper presents a complete sim-to-real pipeline for transferring a traditional Japanese dance from monocular video to a commercial humanoid robot, addressing issues like artifact corruption, state-estimator drift, and reference motion editing, and achieving long-duration, rope-free hardware performances.

Abstract: We present a pipeline for transferring Awa Odori, a traditional Japanese festival dance, from monocular video to a commercial Unitree G1 EDU humanoid. The pipeline combines human mesh recovery, kinematic retargeting, reinforcement learning for motion tracking in simulation, and onboard deployment via ONNX. Training ran on a single RTX 5070 GPU. An eleven-version deployment ladder evaluated on hardware examines three interventions. We detect and repair silent corruption of deployment artifacts, model state-estimator drift during training, and edit the reference motion to support stable execution and a controlled ending. The final policies achieved three consecutive rope-free completions of a 43-second routine, a 46-second performance with automatic handoff, and a continuous 9.7-minute rope-free performance composed from the captured motion. TLDR: A tutorial for multi-robot formation control with differential-drive robots using an overhead camera and ArUco markers, bridging theory and practice with an open-source Python repository.

Abstract: This tutorial presents a step-by-step introduction to multi-robot formation control using differential- drive robots, an overhead camera, and Python. ArUco markers are used to estimate robot positions and orientations, while desired pairwise displacement vectors define a circular formation. By comparing measured and desired relative positions, the controller computes movement vectors that are converted into differential-drive wheel commands, with basic proximity-sensor-based obstacle avoidance. The tutorial guides readers through hardware and software setup, robot pose estimation, formation geometry, feedback control, system integration, and formation-error evaluation. Motivated by the need to bridge the gap between theoretical understanding and practical implementation in robotics education, we emphasize how mathematical concepts are translated into coordinated behaviors on physical robots. Although the implementation uses a specific robotic platform, the underlying perception and formation-control principles can be adapted to other differential-drive robots and simulation environments. The accompanying open-source GitHub repository provides the Python implementation, setup instructions, and demonstrations to support hands-on learning and further exploration of multi-robot systems.
