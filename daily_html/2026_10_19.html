AeroDASH: Asynchronous Vision-Language-Action Control for Continuous UAV Flight VLA

简要说明：AeroDASH提出了一种用于无人机飞行的异步视觉-语言-动作框架，通过B样条轨迹融合和执行感知的GRPO来处理推理延迟，在语言条件飞行任务中显著提高了成功率。

TLDR: AeroDASH introduces an asynchronous VLA framework for UAV flight, using B-spline trajectory fusion and execution-aware GRPO to handle inference latency, achieving significantly higher success rates in language-conditioned flight tasks.

Abstract: Onboard inference latency creates a mismatch between the trajectories predicted by vision-language-action (VLA) policies and the ongoing motion of unmanned aerial vehicles (UAVs). We present AeroDASH, an asynchronous VLA framework for continuous language-conditioned flight. A B-spline policy is initialized through supervised fine-tuning on expert demonstrations, providing continuous trajectory references in position and yaw. During execution, trajectory fusion removes the expired prediction prefix and connects the remaining segment to the active reference with derivative continuity while limiting trajectory deformation. To account for deviations induced by asynchronous execution, execution-aware group relative policy optimization (GRPO) further adapts the policy using trajectory-level rewards. On the UAV-Flow test set, AeroDASH achieves a success rate of 76.56%, exceeding OpenVLA-UAV and AeroDASH w/o GRPO by 38.1 and 20.1 percentage points, respectively. Execution experiments show that AeroDASH sustains motion during inference and reduces acceleration, jerk, and speed fluctuations relative to asynchronous tracking of waypoint sequences. We further demonstrate language-conditioned flight with onboard inference and asynchronous trajectory execution. The code will be released upon acceptance.

TLDR: TILDE introduces a teacher-in-the-loop distillation framework that combines on-policy distillation with autonomous recovery trajectory collection via a learned reward model, significantly improving policy performance over behavior cloning across real-world manipulation tasks.

Abstract: Large-scale action pretraining equips Embodied Foundation Models (EFMs) with strong manipulation capabilities, with particular strengths in generalization and closed-loop robustness, but reproducing such pretraining requires substantial robot data and computation. To transfer these capabilities from pretrained EFMs to policies without action pretraining, we introduce TILDE (Teacher-In-the-Loop Distillation from EFMs), which synergizes on-policy distillation at student-induced states with learning from teacher-executed recovery trajectories. We further propose AutoDAgger, an autonomous data aggregation pipeline in which a robotic reward model is utilized to monitor task progress and trigger teacher intervention, thereby collecting recovery experience without human monitoring or manual control. AutoDAgger accelerates recovery-trajectory collection by more than $2\times$ compared with the human-in-the-loop method. By unifying learning from demonstrations, on-policy teacher supervision, and teacher's corrective behavior, TILDE improves task performance by up to $4.1\times$ over behavior cloning across three real-world tasks and supports effective cross-architecture distillation.

VLA-Rolling: Fewer Denoising Steps, Better Control via Adaptive Action Reuse VLA

简要说明：本文提出VLA-Rolling，一种推理时框架，通过复用流匹配VLA模型中未执行的action tail作为下一步去噪的初始化，并自适应分配去噪预算，在多个VLA模型和基准上降低了推理成本并提升了成功率。

TLDR: This paper introduces VLA-Rolling, an inference-time framework that reuses the unexecuted action tail from flow-matching VLA models as initialization for the next denoising step, adaptively allocating denoising budgets to reduce inference cost while improving success rates across multiple VLA models and benchmarks.

Abstract: Flow-matching vision-language-action (VLA) models generate an action chunk by iteratively denoising Gaussian noise, but typically execute only a short prefix before replanning with updated observations. Under this receding-horizon paradigm, the unexecuted remainder---the action tail---is discarded, and the next chunk is regenerated from fresh noise. We show that this discarded tail retains transferable denoising progress: action-tail initialization can match or outperform a native 10-step Gaussian baseline using fewer denoising steps. However, the optimal denoising budget varies substantially across samples, indicating that tail reuse should be adapted to the updated environment. Based on this observation, we introduce VLA-Rolling, a lightweight inference framework that reuses action tails through three stages: action-tail inheritance, per-action confidence estimation, and confidence-guided refinement through renoising and denoising budget allocation, while the underlying VLA remains frozen. Across $π_{0.5}$, SmolVLA, X-VLA, and MolmoAct2 on LIBERO, MetaWorld, LIBERO-Plus, and RoboTwin 2.0, VLA-Rolling reduces action-chunk inference cost in all 16 model--benchmark pairs, achieving up to a $1.48\times$ speedup, while improving success rate in 14 pairs by up to $15.08$ percentage points. These results show that action tails are not merely discarded predictions, but reusable intermediate states that enable more efficient and accurate VLA inference. These results demonstrate that the action tail can support the generation of better actions with fewer denoising steps. Our project is available at https://chenbao123ww.github.io/VLA-Rolling/.

摘要（中文）：流程匹配视觉-语言-动作（VLA）模型通过迭代去噪高斯噪声来生成动作块，但通常只执行一个短前缀，然后在更新观测后重新规划。在这种滚动时域范式下，未执行的部分——即动作尾部——被丢弃，下一块则从新的噪声中重新生成。我们发现，这个被丢弃的尾部保留了可迁移的去噪进展：使用动作尾部初始化可以在更少的去噪步数下达到或超越原生10步高斯基线。然而，最优去噪预算在不同样本之间差异显著，这表明尾部复用应适应更新后的环境。基于这一观察，我们提出了VLA-Rolling，一种轻量级推理框架，通过三个阶段复用动作尾部：动作尾部继承、逐动作置信度估计，以及通过重新加噪和去噪预算分配进行置信度引导的精细化处理，而底层VLA保持冻结。在LIBERO、MetaWorld、LIBERO-Plus和RoboTwin 2.0上对$π_{0.5}$、SmolVLA、X-VLA和MolmoAct2的实验中，VLA-Rolling在所有16个模型-基准组合中降低了动作块推理成本，最高实现$1.48\times$加速，同时在14个组合中提升了成功率，最高提升$15.08$个百分点。这些结果表明，动作尾部不仅仅是丢弃的预测，而是可复用的中间状态，能够实现更高效、更准确的VLA推理。这些结果也证明，动作尾部能够以更少的去噪步数支持生成更好的动作。我们的项目可在https://chenbao123ww.github.io/VLA-Rolling/获取。

简要说明：DiPLO 提出了一种自蒸馏扩散规划器与全身跟踪策略相结合的人形机器人移动操作框架，通过自蒸馏和强化学习微调提高了任务成功率，并在 Unitree G1 机器人上实现了真实世界部署。

TLDR: DiPLO introduces a self-distilled diffusion planner combined with a whole-body tracking policy for humanoid loco-manipulation, improving task success via self-distillation and RL fine-tuning, with successful real-world deployment on a Unitree G1 robot.

Abstract: Humanoid loco-manipulation is particularly challenging because it requires coordinating locomotion, balance, and object interaction, making it difficult to learn from limited demonstrations or from scratch through Reinforcement Learning (RL). We introduce DiPLO (self-distilled Diffusion Planning for Loco-manipulation), a framework that combines a self-distilled diffusion planner with a low-level whole-body tracking policy. Our planner is pretrained via supervised learning on motions derived from human-to-humanoid retargeting, while the tracker is trained via RL to execute them. DiPLO centers around two complementary post-training stages. First, we introduce Self-Distillation Diffusion Policy Optimisation (SD:DPO) to remove the planner's dependence on strong task conditioning, which we identify as a crucial ingredient for effective pretraining, as downstream fine-tuning does not recover comparable performance when it is omitted.. We then fine-tune this distilled model with Diffusion-Augmented Proximal Policy Optimization (DA:PPO), a maximum-entropy RL method for diffusion models, to improve task success and align generated plans with the frozen tracker's execution capabilities. Together, these stages enable motion generation through state-history and goal conditioning alone, enabling 99% task success in pick-carry-place tasks, compared to just 45% for the pretrained model. The resulting planner also generates motions for unseen object goals, with further evaluations on kicking and pushing/pulling tasks. Finally, we deploy our pipeline on the hardware, using our DA:PPO planner-tracker on a Unitree G1, achieving repeated successful executions without any real-world fine-tuning.

TLDR: RCRA is an online adaptation framework that modulates a frozen conservative navigation policy via LoRA residuals based on estimated pedestrian reactivity, improving navigation efficiency and safety under human-robot distribution shift.

Abstract: Pedestrians differ in whether they respond to a robot, yet many RL policies assume all are non-reactive to prioritize safety. Policies trained without conditioning on local pedestrians' reactivity may therefore be unnecessarily conservative or rely on cooperation that does not occur. We propose RCRA, an online adaptation framework that modulates a learned residual around a frozen conservative navigation policy according to estimated pedestrian reactivity. A Transformer-based estimator infers latent pedestrian reactivity from motion histories and robot context, providing a more accurate signal for navigation adaptation and improving scenario-averaged simulation accuracy by $12.62$ percentage points over the Bayesian baseline, which assumes known pedestrian goals. These estimates are spatially aggregated into an adaptation scale that gates LoRA modules, enabling cooperative behavior near reactive pedestrians and conservative behavior near non-reactive pedestrians. Among baselines achieving SR above $90\%$ in every scenario, RCRA reduces scenario-averaged path length by $16.7\%$ relative to the conservative backbone and by $7.3\%$ relative to randomized-training baselines. It improves mean SR by $14.88$ percentage points over the reactive-trained policy.

Abstract: For practical motion generation, it is essential to handle multimodality (multiple actions can be correct for the same observation) while maintaining low inference cost and strong generalization from limited demonstrations. We introduce an attentive mixture-density policy trained with the Energy Score that represents future visuomotor trajectories as explicit probability distributions with a single forward pass. Concretely, we output Gaussian mixture parameters for joint commands (and optionally attention points) and optimize them with the Energy Score (computed as a sum of 1D CRPS under a diagonal covariance assumption) instead of the negative log-likelihood (NLL) to avoid MDN singularities and gradient-scale instabilities. Our architecture couples explicit spatial attention with mixture-density outputs, enabling robust multimodal behavior selection under visual noise and small-data regimes. On an 8-DOF manipulator, the method generates distinct avoidance trajectories and stabilizes learning in obstacle avoidance.
